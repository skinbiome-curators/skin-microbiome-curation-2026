## 1. Skin site grouping (sebaceous / moist / dry) + dominant genera
**Verdict: CONFIRMED — but NOT by the Byrd 2018 abstract. Cite Grice & Segre 2011 and/or Grice et al. 2009 instead.**

- Byrd AL, Belkaid Y, Segre JA. "The human skin microbiome." 2018. *Nature Reviews Microbiology*. PMID 29332945, DOI 10.1038/nrmicro.2017.157.
  The abstract does **not** state the three site types nor which genus dominates which site. The only relevant line is [ABSTRACT]: "We discuss recent insights into skin microbial communities, including their composition in health and disease, the dynamics between species and interactions with the immune system, with a focus on Propionibacterium acnes, Staphylococcus epidermidis and Staphylococcus aureus."
  Full text is not open in PMC, so the site-dependence statement in this paper is **unverified**. Do not cite Byrd 2018 for this claim without checking the PDF.

- **Preferred citation:** Grice EA, Segre JA. "The skin microbiome." 2011. *Nature Reviews Microbiology* 9:244–253. PMID 21407241, PMCID PMC3535073, DOI 10.1038/nrmicro2537.
  [FULL TEXT] "Our group and others have demonstrated that colonization of bacteria is dependent on the physiology of the skin site, with specific bacteria being associated with moist, dry and sebaceous microenvironments"
  [FULL TEXT] "Propionibacterium spp. are the dominant organisms in these and other sebaceous areas, which confirms classical microbiological studies that describe Propionibacterium spp. as lipophilic residents of the pilosebaceous unit."
  [FULL TEXT] "Metagenomic analysis has revealed that Staphylococcus and Corynebacterium spp. are the most abundant organisms colonizing moist areas"

- **Primary data citation:** Grice EA, Kong HH, Conlan S, et al. "Topographical and temporal diversity of the human skin microbiome." 2009. *Science* 324:1190–1192. PMID 19478181, PMCID PMC2805064, DOI 10.1126/science.1171700.
  [ABSTRACT] "Our analysis of 16S ribosomal RNA gene sequences obtained from 20 distinct skin sites of healthy humans revealed that physiologically comparable sites harbor similar bacterial communities. The complexity and stability of the microbial community are dependent on the specific characteristics of the skin site."
  [FULL TEXT] "We compared the relative abundance of major bacterial groups ... relative to three microenvironment types: (i) sebaceous ...; (ii) moist ...; and (iii) dry ..."
  [FULL TEXT] "Propionibacteria species and Staphylococci species predominated in sebaceous sites"
  [FULL TEXT] "Corynebacteria species predominated in moist sites, although Staphylococci species were also represented"
  [FULL TEXT] "In general, sebaceous sites were less diverse, less even, and less rich than moist and dry sites"

- Supporting (metagenomic): Oh J, Byrd AL, Deming C, et al. "Biogeography and individuality shape function in the human skin metagenome." 2014. *Nature* 514:59–64. PMID 25279917, PMCID PMC4185404, DOI 10.1038/nature13786.
  [ABSTRACT] "metagenomic analyses of diverse body sites in healthy humans demonstrate that local biogeography and strong individuality define the skin microbiome."
  [FULL TEXT] "18 skin sites representing diverse physiologic characteristics ... were sampled: moist (...), dry (...), sebaceous (...), and toenail"
  Note: the Oh 2014 abstract itself names no genera; use Grice for the genus-level statement.

## 2. C. acnes lipase hydrolyzes sebum triglycerides → free fatty acids; C. acnes produces propionic acid / SCFAs
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (two separate sources needed).**

(a) Lipase / triglyceride hydrolysis / acidic pH — Grice EA, Segre JA. 2011. *Nat Rev Microbiol*. PMID 21407241, PMC3535073, DOI 10.1038/nrmicro2537.
  [FULL TEXT] "Full genome sequencing of P. acnes has revealed multiple genes encoding lipases that degrade skin lipids of sebum"
  [FULL TEXT] "P. acnes hydrolyses the triglycerides present in sebum, releasing free fatty acids onto the skin. The bacterium can then adhere to these free fatty acids, and this perhaps aids in the colonization of the sebaceous gland. These free fatty acids also contribute to the acidic pH (~5) of the skin surface"
  (This is a review; the underlying primary refs are its refs 19–21.)

(b) Propionic acid / SCFA production with antimicrobial activity — Shu M, Wang Y, Yu J, Kuo S, Coda A, Jiang Y, Gallo RL, Huang CM. "Fermentation of Propionibacterium acnes, a commensal bacterium in the human skin microbiome, as skin probiotics against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus." 2013. *PLoS ONE* 8:e55380. PMID 23405142, PMCID PMC3566139, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0055380.
  [ABSTRACT] "we demonstrate that fermentation of glycerol with Propionibacterium acnes (P. acnes), a skin commensal bacterium, can function as a skin probiotic for in vitro and in vivo growth suppression of USA300, the most prevalent community-acquired methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (CA-MRSA)."
  [FULL TEXT] "Propionibacterium acnes was so-named for its ability to ferment carbohydrates to propionic acid, a SCFA known to have antimicrobial activity"
  [FULL TEXT] "P. acnes produces propionic acid as the end product of its anaerobic fermentation"

(c) Optional supporting review (strain-level lipase): van Steensel MAM. "The Evolving Microbial Paradigm in Acne." 2026. *Biomolecules*. PMID 41897366, PMC13023635, DOI 10.3390/biom16030430.
  [ABSTRACT] "Unlike commensal strains, phylotype IA1 has high lipase activity, hydrolyzing sebum triglycerides into free fatty acids, specifically palmitic acid."

## 10. Johnson et al. 2019 — 16S sub-regions (V4) fail at species-level resolution
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

Johnson JS, Spakowicz DJ, Hong BY, Petersen LM, Demkowicz P, Chen L, Leopold SR, Hanson BM, Agresta HO, Gerstein M, Sodergren E, Weinstock GM. "Evaluation of 16S rRNA gene sequencing for species and strain-level microbiome analysis." 2019. *Nature Communications* 10:5029. PMID 31695033, PMCID PMC6834636, DOI 10.1038/s41467-019-13036-1.

[ABSTRACT] "We demonstrate that targeting of 16S variable regions with short-read sequencing platforms cannot achieve the taxonomic resolution afforded by sequencing the entire (~1500 bp) gene."
[ABSTRACT] "we demonstrate that appropriate treatment of full-length 16S intragenomic copy variants has the potential to provide taxonomic resolution of bacterial communities at species and strain level."
[FULL TEXT] "Using an in-silico dataset of sequences taken from public databases we show that commonly targeted 16S sub-regions, such as V4, are unable to match the taxonomic accuracy achieved when sequencing the full 16S gene."
[FULL TEXT] "The V4 region performed worst, with 56% of in-silico amplicons failing to confidently match their sequence of origin at this taxonomic level."
[FULL TEXT, Staphylococcus-specific] "the V6–V9 region was notably the best sub-region for classifying sequences belonging to the genera Clostridium and Staphylococcus"
